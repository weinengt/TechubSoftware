## Why does overselling happen during a live sale?

Overselling is usually not about carelessness. It happens when orders arrive faster than anyone can count them.

- **Orders come in as comments.** Buyers type a product code, and the comments scroll past in seconds.
- **Many buyers order at the same moment.** When you say "buy now", nobody can tell who was first for the last piece.
- **Stock is counted by hand.** An admin marks orders in a notebook or spreadsheet, often after the live.
- **More than one person confirms orders.** Two admins can promise the last piece to two buyers.
- **The same stock sells in two places.** If you go live on TikTok and Facebook, both crowds may claim the same items.

Every oversold order ends with an apology, a refund or a long wait for restock. Buyers lose trust, and your team spends time sorting it out.

## How does a live selling system prevent overselling?

A live selling system moves ordering from the comments to a sale page. You share the link during your live, and buyers order on their phones.

Three things then do the work:

1. **Stock is checked and deducted for every order.** It happens the moment the order is placed.
2. **An order queue keeps every order in line.** When a big crowd orders at once, each order is processed in turn. The same piece never goes to two buyers.
3. **Items close automatically when sold out.** Buyers see how many are left, and nobody can order stock you do not have.

| | Orders in comments | Orders on a sale page |
|---|---|---|
| Stock count | By hand, often after the live | Checked and deducted on every order |
| The last piece | Whoever the admin spots first | The first order in the queue |
| Sold out items | The host has to announce it | The item closes by itself |
| Payment | Chased one by one after the live | Paid at checkout |

## What else should a live selling system in Malaysia do?

Stopping overselling is the main job. These features make each live easier to run and learn from.

- **A selling date and time for each product.** Add photos, price and stock before the live, then choose when each product goes on sale.
- **Online payment at checkout.** Buyers pay through a payment gateway, right after they order. The money goes straight into your own merchant account.
- **Visitors by page.** See how many people are on your shop, and which page each is on. It shows you the right moment to push a product.
- **Approval for price changes during a live.** In the rush, a wrong price is easy to key in. An approval step keeps prices in the owner's hands.
- **Reports on best sellers and conversion.** After each live, see what sold, how many visitors bought and your peak minutes. Use this to plan stock for the next live.

## How to run your next live without overselling

1. Count your real stock and enter it for each product before you go live.
2. Set each product's selling date and time to match your live plan.
3. Share your sale page link on the live, and repeat it often. Ask buyers to order there, not in the comments.
4. Watch the visitor count, and talk about the products people are viewing.
5. After the live, check your best sellers and conversion before you restock.
