Beauty package management works best when every package, membership and session lives in one beauty appointment system, not on paper cards or spreadsheets. Each visit deducts a session at checkout, so your staff and your customer always see the same balance.

This guide is for beauty centres, facial and aesthetic centres, and lash, brow, nail and slimming studios in Malaysia.

## Why do paper cards and spreadsheets cause package disputes?

Paper cards get lost, stamps get missed, and a stamp shows no date or beautician. Spreadsheets depend on someone typing in every session, often after closing, so busy days leave gaps.

Then one day, the customer's count and yours do not match. You either give away a free session or send home an unhappy customer.

| | Paper card | Spreadsheet | Booking system |
|---|---|---|---|
| When it is updated | When someone stamps it | When someone types it in | At checkout, every visit |
| Who can see it | Whoever holds the card | Whoever opens the file | Your staff, on any device |
| Proof in a dispute | A stamp with no date | A cell anyone can change | Date, beautician and who recorded it |

## Beauty package management: what should you record?

Take a 10-session facial package as an example. After the fourth visit, anyone at your counter should see 4 used and 6 left, with the date and beautician for each one.

For every package you sell, record:

- Customer name and phone number
- Treatment and total sessions
- Amount paid and payment method
- Date sold, and expiry date if you set one
- Who sold it, for commission
- Each session used: date, beautician and notes
- Sessions left

Put your terms in writing before you sell: how long the package is valid, whether family can share it, and how rescheduling works.

## What should a beauty centre membership system do?

A package is a set number of sessions for one treatment. A membership usually means the customer pays upfront for credit, then enjoys member prices.

A good beauty centre membership system should:

1. Keep the credit balance on the customer's profile.
2. Apply member prices at checkout automatically.
3. Deduct each visit and show the new balance.
4. Keep a history of every top-up and deduction.

Since members pay before the service, ask your accountant how to record that money.

## Face charts, photos and consent at every session

A package can run for weeks, sometimes with different beauticians. Good notes tell the next beautician what was done last time.

A digital face chart lets the beautician mark areas such as an oily T-zone, add before and after photos, and write treatment notes. Skin notes and allergies stay on the profile for every visit. Customer photos are private, so limit who can see them.

Have the customer sign consent before the treatment, and keep it with that visit's record. Unsure what the form should cover? Ask your adviser.

## WhatsApp reminders, deposits and what customers see

A package only keeps customers happy if they actually use it. A WhatsApp reminder when the next session is due brings them back on time.

What your customer should see:

1. A booking confirmation on WhatsApp, showing the sessions left on their package.
2. A reminder before the visit, with one tap to confirm or reschedule.
3. An online deposit when they book, by online banking (FPX), card or e-wallet, depending on your payment gateway.
4. The same session count at your counter as on their phone.

Deposits matter most for new customers and one-off treatments, since package customers have already paid. They also help cut no-shows.

## How should staff commission on package sales work?

Packages are big sales, so staff care about commission. Write the rule down before you launch a package.

Who earns commission on the sale? Does the beautician doing each session earn anything? Clear rules stop arguments at payroll time.

Record who sold each package at the moment of sale. Then each beautician can check their own commission without asking.

### How do I track beauty package sessions?
Record each package on the customer's profile and deduct one session at every visit. A beauty appointment system does this at checkout, so staff and customers always see the same balance.

### What is the difference between a beauty package and a membership?
A package is a set number of sessions for one treatment, while a membership usually means prepaid credit with member prices. If you offer both, keep sessions and credit on the same customer profile.
